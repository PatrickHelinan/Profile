Carbon data audit for a post-precipitation separation stage

Same scenario as Case A, now as an emissions ledger per 1000 m³ treated. Electricity is estimated from nameplate power × load factor × running hours. Every emission factor carries its source, year, region, boundary and a verification status. A factor I could not verify is a gap: the line is shown as missing and the total is flagged incomplete, rather than filled with a guess.

Boundary: separation stage only. Upstream calcium precipitation and the CaF₂ sludge itself are common to both routes and excluded; only route-specific additional solids are counted. A chemical separation stage has no biological CH₄/N₂O source, so Scope 1 is reported as an explicit zero with the reason, not omitted. This is a comparative screening, not an organisational inventory or a full LCA.

Method, formulas and what this is not
  • Electricity: kWh = nameplate kW × load factor × running hours per 1000 m³. Scope 2 = kWh × grid factor (location-based).
  • Nanocoagulant make-up: kg = dose (g/m³) × 1000 m³ / 1000 × (1 − reuse fraction). The reuse fraction is a per-cycle recovery assumption, not the manuscript's "65.9 % of initial inventory entering Cycle 3".
  • Fe₃O₄ bottom-up estimate: FeCl₂ + 2 FeCl₃ + 8 NaOH → Fe₃O₄ + 8 NaCl + 4 H₂O. Per kg Fe₃O₄: 1.40 kg FeCl₃, 0.55 kg FeCl₂, 1.38 kg NaOH stoichiometric (+20 % excess). Factors: FeCl₃ INCOPA iron-oxide route on an anhydrous basis (0.395/0.40); FeCl₂ proxied by FeCl₃ per kg Fe (no FeCl₂ factor found); NaOH Euro Chlor 0.86. Energy: 20 L process water heated 25→80 °C, 3 kg cake water evaporated per kg solid, 80 % heat efficiency, 0.5 kWh/kg auxiliaries, all as grid electricity. Excludes silica coating, packaging, transport and brine treatment. Default ≈ 5.3; 4.3–6.5 across low/high assumption sets.
  • Reference nameplates: mixers from Camp–Stein P = G²μV with textbook G (rapid mix 600 s⁻¹, flocculation/contact 50 s⁻¹) and stage volume V = Qτ from Case A; pumps from ρgQH/η (η = 0.5; recirculation 5 % Q, sludge 1 % Q, 10 m); shaft power ÷ 0.85 drive efficiency rounded up to the next IEC motor size, load factor = required ÷ nameplate. Drum drive 0.75 kW at 0.6 and scraper 0.37 kW at 0.8 are reference assumptions. Feed pumping is common to both routes and excluded.
  • Carbon break-even: reuse at which route M equals route C: make-up allowed = (C total − M non-chemical) ÷ fFe₃O₄; reuse = 1 − make-up ÷ dose.
  • Fe₃O₄ precursor lower bound: INCOPA FeCl₃ (iron-oxide route) 0.395 kg CO₂e per kg of 13.8 % Fe product → 2.862 kg CO₂e/kg Fe; Fe₃O₄ is 72.36 % Fe → 2.07 kg CO₂e/kg Fe₃O₄. Excludes NaOH, heating, washing, drying, nano-processing and any silica coating. It is a floor, not an estimate.
  • Hydroxide sludge: Fe(OH)₃ dry = anhydrous FeCl₃ × 106.87/162.20; anhydrous = 0.40 × product mass. Wet tonnes = dry / dry-solids fraction.
  • Scope 1: IPCC 2019 Refinement Vol. 5 Ch. 6 covers biological treatment; a chemical separation stage has no process CH₄/N₂O source. Reported as 0 with this reason.
  • Not included: upstream precipitation chemicals (CaCl₂), equipment embodied carbon, silica-coating reagents, downstream water reuse credits, uncertainty ranges on factors. GWP basis follows each source (INCOPA: IPCC 2007 GWP100; grid factors: CO₂ only).
  • Self-check: on load the page recomputes fixed vectors (kWh arithmetic, FeCl₃ line, lower-bound derivation, gap handling) and compares with the Python test suite's expectations.
  • AI use: drafted with AI assistance; factor lookups were made against the sources listed and their status recorded honestly, including the ones that could not be opened.
